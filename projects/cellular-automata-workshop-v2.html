Pixels That Think

160 students • 4 weeks • Summer 2025

A 4-hour workshop on cellular automata for high school students, run through the Ashoka Young Scholars Programme. 160 students from grades 9 to 12 across four weeks in May and June 2025.

The goal: teach computation through Conway's Game of Life, starting from paper and ending with working code. No prior programming required.

Approach

We started without screens. Students stood in a grid holding cards (white for alive, black for dead) and manually applied the neighbour rules to each other. This made the logic physical before anyone touched a keyboard.

From there, we moved to a custom p5.js simulation projected onto the ceiling while students worked on laptops below. The ceiling projection was a simple jig Ojas Tripathi built from two planks of wood and plywood.

The structure: 1D Wolfram rules first to build intuition, then the full 2D Game of Life. By day two, students were filling in scaffolded code templates (grid initialisation, neighbour counting, rule application) and leaving with working simulations.

Co-teaching

I co-taught with Yesirat Sanni, a third-year Mathematics and Computer Science student interning with both the Makerspace and Young Scholars Programme. The plan was for me to lead most sessions while she assisted.

By the second week, Yesirat was leading the majority: explaining rules, debugging student code, running the unplugged demonstrations. I handled the simulation tool and the code-along portions.

Technical Notes

  • Toroidal topology: The grid wraps. Cells at the right edge neighbour cells on the left, using modular arithmetic: (x + i + cols) % cols
  • Double-buffering: Two grids, swapped each frame ([grid, nextGrid] = [nextGrid, grid]) to avoid corrupting state mid-update
  • Rule 110: One of the 256 elementary 1D automata, proven Turing-complete. We used this to show that simple local rules can compute anything
  • Rule 90: Generates the Sierpinski triangle. Good for showing how fractals emerge from cellular automata
